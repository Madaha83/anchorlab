ANCHOR: Awareness, Needs, and Competencies for Human and Organizational Resources

Better ways to measure, develop, and support people at work.

The ANCHOR Lab studies fair and accurate assessment, leadership, motivation and recovery, and workplace learning. We do careful science on practical questions, with students as full members of the team.

For students

What students do here

Our founding cohort joined in Fall 2026. Applications are open for future cohorts.

Lab members work on real research, from designing a study to presenting what it found. You can join for 1 to 3 credits a semester through MGT 491 (fall) or MGT 492 (spring) Directed Study, or as a volunteer, and you don’t need prior research experience. New to research? That’s who this is for.

  • Design studiesBuild surveys and experiments on leadership, motivation, and training.
  • Work with dataCollect, clean, and analyze data in R or SPSS.
  • Share findingsHelp prepare posters, presentations, and papers.

Current and recent work

Under review

Practice, label, or placebo? Mindfulness in training

A 2×2 experiment in an air traffic control simulation that separates the effects of actual mindfulness practice from simply calling an activity “mindfulness.”

Training and workplace learning

In progress

Need satisfaction at work and away from it

A two-study project on whether meeting psychological needs outside work can make up for needs unmet at work. Study 1, a time-lagged survey, is being revised. Study 2, a daily diary study built with lab students, is in preparation for ethics review.

Motivation, needs, and recovery

In development

AI-assisted assessment and feedback

Building on a proof-of-concept platform, this project will test whether large language models paired with validated assessments can give accurate, personalized developmental feedback at scale.

Leadership competencies and AI-assisted feedback

In development

Validity and fairness in leader assessment

A planned line of studies on whether leadership assessments predict outcomes equally well across groups, and how to make results clearer for the people who use them.
